# Discard Warlock strategy

This is the active decision policy. Historical examples are regression evidence,
not extra rules with equal priority. Current legal options, public state and card
text determine what is possible; the planner does not invent draws or random bodies.

## Decision priority

1. Reject known fatal self-damage, draw/fatigue and attacks. Account for effective
   Health, Armor, observed Immunity, hand space and visible enemy damage. Survival
   and legal execution precede mana-spending or value preferences.
2. Take a fully supported immediate or planned lethal. Do not delay lethal to
   develop more buff recipients or acquire optional information.
3. Compare expiring development before losing a useful Temporary card. A discovered
   Party Fiend costing one can build three bodies without Coin; at three effective
   Health its Battlecry is fatal. Expiry does not justify suicide or any weak play.
4. Decide WHETHER information is part of the turn. A legal draw is not a draw intent.
   Resolve a chosen safe discovery/draw before committing resources when it preserves
   the intended setup mana. Reobserve the real hand afterwards; never queue unknown
   cards. A selected free Catacombs discovery normally precedes a weapon commitment.
5. Compare complete development and buff intents, including discard-trigger summons.
   Apply buffs before attacks that benefit from them. An attack that creates bodies,
   removes a Taunt or frees necessary slots can belong to setup and occur earlier.

Spend mana when it buys useful development, trades, pressure or resources. Keeping
mana or ending is preferable to a fatal or low-value play. A mandatory draw next
turn is part of the survival/resource calculation.

## Development and buffs

First evaluate whether buffing is worthwhile this turn. Price the actual eligible
recipients, immediate attack/trade gain and survival against alternative development
and refill. Three +1/+1 recipients yield +3 Attack/+3 Health; the user's approximate
mana valuations are coaching comparisons, not exact card values or a mandatory cast.
A lone sleeping recipient without a concrete trade, lethal or survival benefit
usually wastes a permanent buff. Single-recipient buffs are not universally banned.
Dormant minions are ineligible; a ready zero-Attack egg may become an attacker only
after a buff, while a newly summoned egg still cannot attack.

If the chosen turn includes a buff, first complete feasible development that adds
recipients while preserving buff mana and survival. There is no minimum existing
body count and no requirement to fill seven slots. Count locations against slots.
Immediate summons count; Deathrattle or delayed summons do not become recipients
merely because their source is played. Do not bypass immediate lethal or a concrete
survival/trade benefit just to develop more bodies.

Compare direct plays, Coin-enabling plays, targeted discard and weapon attack
triggers as whole intents. Do not hide a playable discard payoff solely because an
outlet is in hand. On five bodies with two slots, Acolytes plus buff can be better
than Occultist discarding Acolytes: the outlet takes a slot before its summons.
On a thin board, Occultist discarding Walking Dead can establish two Taunts when
room exists; at three mana this can beat a one-body buff, while four mana permits
comparison of development plus buff.

combinationEvidence is a bounded feasibility proof, not a guarantee of winning or
an unconditional command. Its exact prefix stops at unknown summons, draws, choices
or triggers. Random bodies have count bounds, not invented identities, stats, Taunt
or buff eligibility. Reserve the conditional buff cost, observe and replan.

For highest-cost discard weapons, compare these cases using the shared evidence:
- A safe hero attack uniquely discards Acolytes and preserves buff mana: develop via
  weapon, attack, observe random summons, then reconsider buff before original
  minion attacks. Equipment alone does not trigger the discard. The user's forced
  first-step ruling requires a surviving combo proof and no uncertain secret.
- It discards Acolytes but leaves no buff mana: compare weapon plus discard against
  directly playing Acolytes plus buff if affordable. Do not describe an impossible
  post-weapon buff as part of the executable plan.
- Another card is highest: compare its actual discard payoff and safe removal of
  a 3/4-Health target against direct development plus buff. A highest-cost tie is
  random, not targeted. Frozen/exhausted heroes cannot supply the trigger.

## Opponent objective and trades

Use matchupObjective's public evidence and mode. Class probabilities and their
confidence are local sample estimates, not identified archetypes or calibrated
accuracy. Unknown/conflicting evidence keeps a neutral objective. Control and combo
share the pressure opening strategy; later revealed quests, removal and recovery can
change the current objective. No fixed T5/T6 combo deadline is assumed.

Against control/combo pressure windows, convert cards and turns into attacking
bodies, useful multi-body buffs and effective face damage before stabilization.
Compare a trade's protection of future attacks with the face damage sacrificed.
Clear Taunts, engines or lethal threats when this enables the pressure plan; do
not clear harmless bodies by reflex. An empty board alone does not identify control.

Against board opponents, efficient exchanges, survival and continuing resources
can outweigh this turn's face damage. A safe modest board lead and thin hand can
justify refill instead of a buff. When behind, compare discard summons, useful
removal and Duke's tagged Rush size; do not assume an aggressive deck lacks removal.

Effect applicability, evidence confidence and effect strength are separate. At
identical stats and removal costs, remove a confidently active beneficial engine
before a spent or wrong-zone effect. Patches' deck-only summon is inactive on board;
Ship's Chirurgeon can keep affecting later summons. With different bodies or costs,
compare retaliation, survivors, lost attacks, mana, Deathrattles, aura strength and
opponent trigger opportunities. A strong engine can justify extra resources; its
mere effect icon does not mean it must die at any cost. Unknown effects retain their
full text for judgment. Taunt, Divine Shield, Lifesteal, immunity and lethal matter.

Damage spells may remove valuable threats or contribute to a pressure/lethal plan;
face damage has value beyond an immediate kill, but do not waste finite removal on
face while losing the board. Soul Barrage's random split is not guaranteed targeted
removal. The historical weak-board ruling favors a fixed discard summon over a
speculative split; dense low-Health boards or burn range can change that comparison.
Lumia's damage-triggered hero Immunity limits sequential face hits: remove the effect
if feasible or use the largest effective first hit; do not assume later hits land.

## Information, recovery and temporary cards

Draw costs include hand space after played/discarded cards leave, remaining mana,
ordinary cards left, known Casts When Drawn hazards and the fatigue counter. Known
Shreds can auto-cast and replace themselves through the deck into fatigue without
adding a hand card. Mixed ordinary cards and hazards yield damage bounds, not a
predicted order. Apply the same analysis to Life Tap, direct Gul'dan draw and weapon
attacks discarding Gul'dan. Unknown opponent forced draws are risks, not certain plays.

A Temporary discovery that cannot be cast this turn is valued by its actual discard
payoff. Fixed Walking Dead is usually a reliable board option; extra random Acolyte
bodies can gain value when a feasible buff uses them. Discover offers must include
this turn's affordable combinations, not just isolated cards. Lowest-cost ties remain
random: Continuity before Whispers can remove a cheap competing discard target and
allow Egg's spiders to arrive before Whispers buffs them. Reobserve each stage.

Chamber of Viscidus costs three mana to install and occupies one slot; installation
itself does not draw or summon. Use the actual ready activation/choice options.
A summoning discard payoff can combine development and refill, but the draw is unknown.
After Slime 'em!, evaluate Ectoplasm from the known recovery pool. On an empty board
with room for at least three known recovered bodies, the recorded recovery ruling
precedes ordinary location/refill choices after lethal; unknown pools need comparison.
Resummoning does not trigger Battlecries or grant attacks by itself.

## Opening and mulligan

Seek concrete plays in turns one and two, attacking development and a reliable curve.
A bare 0/2 Boneweb Egg is not active early pressure; an unsupported buff does not
create a board. Four-mana Chronoclaws is not an early Egg outlet. Without support,
replace Egg, buff and slow conditional cards rather than spending Coin just to make
an inert body. Coin is valuable when it unlocks a stronger current line and leaves
reasonable known follow-up; maximizing spent crystals alone is not tempo.

Each retained discard activation supports at most one retained material, regardless
of cheap development. One Occultist cannot activate both Walking Dead and Silverware
Golem. Default to Walking Dead's fixed 2/5 Taunt; Golem's extra Attack against control
is a possible comparison, not a proven universal preference. A supported Whispers
plus material pair can remain; without the retained outlet, replace excess materials.

Use matchupObjective.mulligan.preferences, already blended by decisionWeight: stronger
aggression/tempo evidence favors immediate cheap bodies and reliable exchanges;
control/combo pressure favors attacking development and efficient discard curves.
Low support stays near neutral. Tempo is an aggression proxy, not a confirmed pure
aggro deck. Historical kept-card win rates are conditional observations, not causal
keep rules; they never override an unplayable curve or user-ruled combo constraints.

On empty boards at two mana, Coin plus Occultist discarding a fixed summon can beat
bare Egg when another known three-mana attacking minion supplies next turn's curve.
This ruling requires the actual payoff, no competing cheap play and a surviving
follow-up. Against control, compare a small buff now with a known wider same-turn
burst next turn; preserve Coin when it funds that concrete line, without assuming
the next draw supplies missing pieces or that buffs protect against every AOE.
