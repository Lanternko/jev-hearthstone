# Discard Warlock strategy

Use the fixed 30-card wild Warlock deck. Aim for lethal first; otherwise establish an early board efficiently, then push face damage. Trade for important threats, survival, board space, or profitable exchanges. Avoid unnecessary trades when ahead. Account for taunt, divine shield, rush attack restrictions and immunity.

Before any attack, and before playing a Rush minion to trade, do the arithmetic in both directions. The target dies only if your Attack is at least its remaining Health. Your attacker dies if the target's Attack is at least your attacker's Health. An exchange that satisfies the second but not the first hands over a minion for nothing and is almost never correct. User ruling, 2026-09-20 practice game turn 8: Duke of Below at 4/4 into an Unbound Elemental 4/5 is exactly that losing trade and was rejected; at 6/6 the Duke kills the Elemental and survives, and only then is playing it worth considering.

An enemy minion that generates recurring value on a trigger -- "at the end of your turn", "at the start of your turn", or any per-turn draw or summon -- must be removed, ahead of face damage. Every turn it survives is another card or body for the opponent, so the trade is cheap even when the minion looks harmless. A zero-Attack body such as Mana Tide Totem 0/3 deals no damage back, so attacking it costs nothing at all; spend exactly enough attackers to kill it and send the rest at the face. User correction, 2026-09-20 practice game turn 6: with three 1/1s against a lone Mana Tide Totem 0/3, attacking the hero instead of killing the totem was rejected.

A board-wide +1/+1 needs a board. Play one only when three or more friendly minions are out; or one to two are out, the hand is deep enough and there is nothing else worth doing this turn; or one to two are out, the board is behind, and the buff converts a trade into a favourable one. With an empty board the buff does nothing and the card is thrown away: on turn one of the 2026-09-20 practice game Entropic Continuity was played into an empty board and accomplished nothing, which the user rejected. Cards that also discard or destroy, such as Wicked Whispers, can still be worth playing for that half alone. Lethal and crucial trades override this guideline. Seven board spaces include locations. Multiple summons require spaces; a single large minion is often better near a full board.

Wicked Whispers discards the lowest-cost card before buffing. Remove cheaper cards only when worthwhile to steer toward a two-cost discard payoff. The user's observed combo is discarding the two-minion summon spell, then both new minions receive +1/+1. Ties are random, never targeted. Compare the cost of prematurely using other buffs or Coin.

A weapon is not a one-turn card. Chronoclaws carries three durability, so equipping it buys three hero attacks, and its mana cost is paid off against that whole total rather than a single swing; its discard fires after every one of them, which is repeated value whenever the hand holds discard payoffs. Order the turn around that discard: play the cards worth keeping before swinging, so the weapon burns something expendable instead. A minion already worth putting on the board is almost never worth trading for one hero attack. User ruling, 2026-09-20 practice game turn 10: with Duke of Below at 8/8 the unique highest-cost card in hand, attacking first would have discarded it, and playing it first was correct. The weapon attacks for 4 and then discards the highest-cost card. Recompute highest costs before attacking: remove competing four-cost cards to steer toward Soul Barrage when efficient. If Hand of Gul'dan is uniquely highest, those removals are unnecessary. Soul Barrage distributes damage randomly over all enemies; do not count all five as guaranteed face damage with enemy minions. Below roughly 15 enemy health increase pressure, context permitting.

The location offers three hand cards, not arbitrary access to all cards, and has cooldown. Before both playing it and activating it, count the hand and ask whether cards are actually needed. Two situations justify it: a quality discard-payoff minion is in hand and the three on offer carry high expected value; or the hand is simply poor, in which case a low-value activation is still acceptable as a refill. Outside those, hold it. Soularium can find discard payoffs or supply playable cards with spare mana. Preserve board space for end-turn temporary discards. Never treat draw probabilities as known draws.

Resolve draws and discovers before committing attacks. A drawn buff raises the value of every attack that follows, and an attack once taken cannot be undone, so the free information comes first. A location is also a board slot: spending its last charge destroys it and gives that slot back, which is how you make room for a discard payoff that summons. Sequence accordingly -- location first, then the weapon swing whose discard needs somewhere to land, then attacks. User ruling, 2026-09-20 practice game turn 10: swinging the weapon at a 7/7 board discarded Disposable Acolytes and wasted both summons, and attacking face before using the location cost about five damage because the draw turned up Entropic Continuity.

A temporary card expires at the end of the turn and that expiry counts as a discard, so a discover-and-make-temporary effect such as Cursed Catacombs is free discard fuel, not a wasted card. Having no mana left does not make it worthless. Judge it on expected value: the deck still holding many discard payoffs, and a board with room, both argue for playing it, because the temporary card may be a Walking Dead worth discarding, or a Soul Barrage worth discarding while the opponent has minions to hit. A full board is the clear case against it, since a payoff that summons has nowhere to go. User ruling, 2026-09-20 practice game turn 6: correctly held at 7/7 board, but the reasoning "no mana, so the temporary card is unusable" was wrong.

The expiry discard is real but small, and it is paid for by permanently removing a card from the deck, so it does not on its own justify playing the effect. The odds that three cards off the top of the deck contain the specific payoff worth chasing are not high enough. Play it on a turn with mana to spare, where the discovered card can actually be cast and the discard is a bonus rather than the whole return; with no mana left and one or zero board spaces, hold it instead. User ruling, 2026-09-20 practice game turn 8: playing Cursed Catacombs at zero mana with one space free was wrong even though the expiry did pump Duke of Below.

When a discovered card cannot be cast this turn and will therefore expire, the choice is really "which card do I want deleted from my deck". Take the one that is least useful, or one whose own discard text pays out. Never take a board buff: those decide mid and late game and must stay in the deck. User ruling, same turn: choosing Wicked Whispers over the spare Party Fiend was wrong.

Payoff preferences: Hand of Gul'dan for resources; Walking Dead for survival; eggs/two summons with room and a pending buff; single minions with one space. Accept ordinary discards to secure pressure. Life Tap draws new resources, does not recover discarded cards. Replan after each random effect/draw.

User correction from the first live trial: an opening hand with no low-cost plays must be aggressively replaced to find early tempo. Do NOT keep a four-cost weapon in such a hand. A turn-three discard pair does not justify an entire slow opening. Prefer one-cost board minions, then two-cost proactive plays; keep synergistic discard/payoff pairs only when early tempo is already covered. Keeping all of Ocular Occultist, Chronoclaws and Walking Dead on the play was explicitly rejected by the user. With that exact hand replace all three to find early plays. The remaining matchup-specific mulligan policy is provisional.

Lethal is the first question of every turn, before value, tempo or card advantage. Add up the damage the turn can actually reach the enemy hero with -- every minion that can attack, the hero with a weapon, and burn from spells and expiry discards -- and compare it to Health plus Armour. If it is enough, take it and ignore everything else on this page. A draw-three or a board buff that wins next turn is worth nothing against a line that wins now. User ruling, 2026-09-20 practice game turn 14: Jev chose Hand of Gul'dan for three cards over Wicked Whispers, which was exactly lethal, because no single option stated the sum. The damage clock now appears in every option description for that reason.

Count 0-Attack minions when pricing a board-wide buff. A minion at 0 Attack generates no attack option at all, so it is invisible in any tally of the turn's damage, and it is easy to evaluate a buff as though it only pumped the minions already swinging. It does not: +1/+1 turns each 0-Attack body into a real attacker. In the same turn 14 two Hexed Frogs at 0/1 became 1/2 and supplied the final two points of a thirteen-point lethal. Transform effects such as Hex are the usual source of these bodies, so a board that has just been answered is exactly where this is missed.

Board width is the other half of a buff's price. With six minions out, a board-wide +1/+1 is worth six stat points on each side plus the attack it unlocks, which beats almost any draw effect even when there is no lethal, because it both raises damage and wins the trades that hold the board. Compare a buff against draw by counting the bodies it touches, not by the card's mana cost. This does not soften the rule above it: with an empty or near-empty board the buff is still thrown away.


The Coin is not a tempo card to spend early. Play it only when one of two conditions holds: the mana left this turn is not enough for the card you want and exactly one more crystal makes it enough; or the Coin itself is the cheapest card in hand and is therefore blocking a discard outlet, in which case spending it moves the discard onto a card that actually pays out. Outside those two cases, hold it. User ruling, 2026-09-20 second live game turn 2: with zero mana left, spending the Coin turned a dead turn into Wicked Whispers discarding a Boneweb Egg -- both conditions at once. Note that a discard outlet reads the hand at resolution time, so the Coin can silently absorb the discard of a card such as Wicked Whispers and waste it entirely; check what the cheapest card in hand actually is before casting the outlet, not after.


Against a human opponent, speed and consistency beat cleverness. Three defaults, in this order. Spend the mana: finish each turn with as little unspent mana as possible, and prefer the line that uses the whole pool over a cheaper line that leaves crystals unused. Hold the board: when a play would trade away your own board position for a small amount of face damage, keep the board instead, because board control is what wins the following turns. Attack with everything: every minion that can attack should attack each turn. The one exception is a Taunt on the opposing board -- there, stop and price the trade, since attacking into Taunt can lose a minion for nothing, and it may be right to send only the bodies that survive the exchange. User ruling, 2026-09-21, first game against a live opponent.


Never spend mana casting a card your own discard outlets want to throw away. Hand of Gul'dan draws three cards when it is played OR discarded, and Soul Barrage pays out from the discard the same way: hard-casting either buys with six mana exactly what a discard would have given for nothing, and it also removes the card that was making the next discard profitable. Leave them in hand as discard fodder and spend the mana on board instead. User ruling, 2026-09-21 turns 4 and 8.

Take the favourable trade before going face. A 2/1 that kills a 3/2 wins the exchange outright, and a body traded up is worth more than two points on the opponent's face. This outranks the habit of attacking face with everything: attack with everything means everything attacks, not that everything attacks the hero. User ruling, 2026-09-21 turn 5.

A Rush minion exists to clear. On the turn it is summoned, price the minions it can kill before considering the hero -- that is the whole reason it was given Rush, and it is the one attacker that can still trade on the turn it lands. The same applies to any large body facing a smaller one: a 10/10 into a 4/6 removes a threat and keeps the 10/10, which is strictly better than the equivalent face damage. User ruling, 2026-09-21 turns 6 and 10.

Life Tap costs two health, and that cost is real once the opponent is within reach of lethal. Count the damage the opposing board plus a plausible burn card can deal next turn before tapping; if tapping puts you inside that number, take the turn without the card. Cards are worthless at zero health. User ruling, 2026-09-21 turn 9.

## Mulligan and tempo, from the second live game (user ruling, 2026-09-21)

Mulligan for the two things this deck cannot function without: a cheap minion to hold the
board on turns 1-2, and discard fodder for the outlets. Keep a Location -- it costs nothing
to hold and pays every turn it is down. When the opening hand has neither a cheap minion nor
fodder, throw everything except the Location back.

The Coin is tempo, not mana. Only play it in a turn where the extra crystal actually buys a
larger play this turn -- Coin into Ocular Occultist on turn 2 is the line. Spending the Coin
without converting it into a bigger board that same turn is a strict error, not a judgement
call: it is one card thrown away for nothing.

Order of operations inside a turn: the body comes down before the buff. If a minion is going
to be hard-cast and then buffed, cast it first -- the buff can miss its target if the board
fills or the minion dies, and casting first never costs anything.

Prefer the discard outlet over the hard cast. Ocular Occultist discarding Walking Dead was
the correct line on turns 3, 4 and 5; hard-casting Disposable Acolytes was not. The outlet
converts a dead card into board presence, so when both lines are available the outlet wins.

Life Tap is off the table once the opponent has lethal on board. Not "a real cost" as the
turn-9 ruling put it -- flatly forbidden. Count the opponent's total board attack against
our health plus the health of our own Taunts, since their swings have to clear the Taunts
before a point reaches the hero. Lethal on board means that total gets through both.
